# Adversarial review brief

You are reviewing for **TMLR**. Two criteria, and only two:

1. Are the claims supported by accurate, convincing and clear evidence?
2. Would some portion of the audience be interested in the findings?

**Novelty and significance are explicitly NOT acceptance criteria.** Do not
score them, and do not recommend strengthening a novelty claim — at this venue
a priority claim buys nothing and creates an obligation. Flag any you find as a
liability, not an asset.

Your job is to find what would make an Action Editor reject this, or what a
referee would demand before acceptance. Be hostile. Prefer a specific,
checkable objection over a general concern.

## What you have

- `PAPER.md` — the manuscript, 11 sections
- `APPENDIX.md` — appendices A–L, generated from the artefacts
- `NUMBERS.txt` — every figure in the paper, regenerated by `verify_paper.py`
- `NUMBERS_E.txt` — Stratum E figures
- `REFERENCES.md`, `PREREG.md`, `SUBMISSION.md` — bibliography, analysis plan,
  and a cover sheet that lists what is submitted and what is knowingly weak

Section references of the form `[N §k]` point into `NUMBERS.txt`. If a number
in the prose disagrees with its cited section, that is a finding — say so.

## Already closed. Do not spend the round here.

Each of these was raised in an earlier review and acted on. Re-reporting them
wastes the pass. Attack the *adequacy* of the fix if you think it is thin, but
do not report the original objection as new.

  published, 120 positives injected by rule, against a plan fixed before any

  test and is therefore narrower than the word in general use, with the three
  consequences that follow.
- **Statistical objections.** §7.4's regression-on-component correlation was
  withdrawn as an artefact and the withdrawal is stated in the section.
  §6.5 reports Holm–Bonferroni. Single-shuffle rows are marked.
- **Citations.** Two verification rounds. `REFERENCES.md` exists.

## What would be most useful

- A specific number, table or sentence that the evidence does not support.
- A claim whose strength exceeds what its cited `[N §k]` section shows.
- An alternative explanation for the §6.2 subtype result that the paper's
  controls do not exclude.
- A reason an Action Editor would not be persuaded by the Stratum E argument.
- Anything the paper asserts about the world rather than about its own corpus.

Length is a legitimate finding: if a section could be cut without losing a
scientific claim, name it.
